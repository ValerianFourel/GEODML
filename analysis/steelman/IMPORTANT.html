2. Abstract as the evidence supports it today (provisional)

We introduce a method for isolating user intent in generative engine optimization: each prompt's intent is measured as a continuous position between information-seeking and action-ready in embedding space, compared within a topic, and prompts and documents are placed on the same scale. Applying it to a frozen, fully logged LLM search pipeline, we trace how intent moves the results stage by stage. Intent shifts what gets cited mainly by changing which sources reach the shortlist, through the queries the agent writes and the reranker that filters their results, while the generator's choice among shortlisted sources adds almost nothing to the shift. Few action-ready pages exist, which caps how close the cited sources can come to the most action-ready prompts.

The last sentence waits on the supply analysis; the generator sentence waits on Llama; every number waits on the held-out keywords. Contribution 3 as it stands today: "as prompts become action-ready they stop naming the topic keyword and the agent's queries follow"; "the generator's choice among shown sources adds almost nothing to the shift (Qwen)"; "few action-ready pages exist (under 1% of pages sit above the 80th prompt percentile)".
